# Jack Callahan

Systems & infrastructure engineer based in Detroit, MI.

Spent the past twenty-odd years working close to the OS boundary — starting out on Solaris and early Linux boxes in the late '90s, building telecom framing pipelines, network transport stacks, and low-latency daemon processes where tail latency, state-machine invariants, and predictable memory behavior actually matter.

### Day-to-day focus

- **Network & wire protocols**: HTTP/2 & TCP flow-control state machines, non-blocking socket runtimes (`epoll`, `io_uring`), framing codecs, and backpressure accounting.
- **Systems programming**: Rust, C, C++, and Python when gluing test harnesses or trace tooling together.
- **Diagnostics & observability**: `perf`, `bpftrace`, `gdb`, packet captures, and deterministic reproduction harnesses over guesswork.

### Away from the terminal

- Wrenching on classic American V8 iron in the garage, running summer track days out at Waterford Hills, and logging standalone ECU telemetry over CAN bus.
- Restoring 1970s Marantz and McIntosh receiver/tube stages on the bench, spinning old jazz and blues pressings on vinyl, and brewing manual pour-overs with washed Ethiopian beans.
